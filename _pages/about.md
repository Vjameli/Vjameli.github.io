I am PhD student in Operations Research at the [University of Toronto](https://www.utoronto.ca/) supervised by Prof. [Timothy C.Y. Chan](https://chan.mie.utoronto.ca/).
I hold a BSc (Hons) in Applied Mathematics from the [Institute of Mathematics, Statistics and Scientific Computing](https://www.ime.unicamp.br/en) of the [University of Campinas](https://unicamp.br/en/).
My interests lie in both methodological aspects of mathematical optimization and its applications in healthcare.

During my undergraduate studies, I worked on improving and automating treatment planning for external beam radiotherapy.
In the first part of my PhD, I have focused on two seemingly distinct areas that are surprisingly connected: magnet design and computational geometry.

Working with Prof. [Teodor Stanescu](https://radonc.utoronto.ca/faculty/teodor-stanescu) at the Princess Margaret Cancer Centre,
I aim to generalize the theory of optimal magnet design to accommodate arbitrarily complex external magnetic fields.
This would make it possible, for the first time, to design magnets optimally for MRI-LINAC systems and provide the foundation 
for a proof-of-concept of a new MRI-LINAC design.

I also study how geometric intersection graphs can be used to strengthen set-packing constraints.
These graphs appear in many classical problems in Operations Research—including magnet design.
But the literature has only studied intersection graphs that can be decomposed into intervals (intervals, rectangles, and boxes)
to strengthen set-packing constraints. What about more weird shapes?

Looking ahead to the rest of my PhD, I am interested in exploring the connections between inverse optimization, robust optimization, and Markov decision processes (MDPs).
